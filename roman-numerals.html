What Are Roman Numerals? Complete Guide with Examples

Roman numerals are a numeral system originating in ancient Rome, using combinations of letters from the Latin alphabet — I, V, X, L, C, D, and M — to represent numbers. They were the standard writing system for numbers in Europe throughout the Middle Ages, and remain in widespread use today for clocks, Super Bowl numbers, movie sequels, Olympic Games, copyright dates, chapter headings, and tattoos.

The Seven Core Roman Numeral Symbols

The entire Roman numeral system is built from just seven symbols: I (1), V (5), X (10), L (50), C (100), D (500), and M (1,000). Every number from 1 to 3,999 can be expressed using combinations of these seven letters following specific rules about repetition and subtraction.

How to Convert a Number to Roman Numerals — Step by Step

Converting any number to Roman numerals uses a greedy subtraction algorithm. Start with the largest Roman value (M = 1000) and work down to the smallest (I = 1):

Example: Convert 2026 to Roman Numerals
2026 ÷ M(1000) → 2 times → MM, remainder 26
26 ÷ X(10) → 2 times → XX, remainder 6
6 ÷ V(5) → 1 time → V, remainder 1
1 ÷ I(1) → 1 time → I, remainder 0
Result: MMXXVI

The Subtractive Notation Rule

Roman numerals use six specific subtractive pairs to avoid repeating a symbol more than three times. These are: IV (4), IX (9), XL (40), XC (90), CD (400), CM (900). Only the combinations shown above are valid — you cannot write IC for 99 (use XCIX) or VX for 5 (use V). This rule creates unambiguous representations for all numbers.

Roman Numerals in Modern Life

Today, Roman numerals appear in many contexts familiar to people across the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, Germany, and France. The Super Bowl is numbered in Roman numerals (e.g., Super Bowl LVII = 57). Movie sequels and series use them (Rocky II, Star Wars Episode IV). Clock faces traditionally show hours in Roman numerals from I to XII. Wedding anniversaries, building cornerstones, and legal documents use Roman numeral dates for a sense of formality and timelessness.

Frequently Asked Questions — Roman Numeral Converter

How do I convert a number to Roman numerals? ▾
To convert a number to Roman numerals, break it down using the values M=1000, CM=900, D=500, CD=400, C=100, XC=90, L=50, XL=40, X=10, IX=9, V=5, IV=4, I=1. Subtract the largest possible value, write the symbol, then repeat. For example: 2026 = MM + XX + VI = MMXXVI. Our free online converter above does this automatically and shows a full breakdown.
What is MMXXVI in Roman numerals? ▾
MMXXVI equals 2026. Breakdown: MM = 2000, XX = 20, VI = 6. So MM + XX + VI = 2026. This is the current year 2026 in Roman numerals, commonly seen in building cornerstones, movie credits, coin designs, and graduation commemorations.
What is the Roman numeral for 2024, 2025, and 2026? ▾
2024 = MMXXIV, 2025 = MMXXV, 2026 = MMXXVI. These conversions are popular for tattoos, graduation announcements, class rings, commemorative coins, and building dedications. Use our year chips above to instantly convert any recent year.
What is XLVIII in numbers? ▾
XLVIII = 48. XL = 40 (50 minus 10), V = 5, III = 3. So 40 + 5 + 3 = 48. Super Bowl XLVIII was played in 2014 between the Seattle Seahawks and Denver Broncos.
How do you read Roman numerals on a clock? ▾
On a Roman numeral clock face: I=1, II=2, III=3, IV=4, V=5, VI=6, VII=7, VIII=8, IX=9, X=10, XI=11, XII=12. Note: many classic clock faces use "IIII" instead of "IV" for 4 — this is a historical watchmaker's convention. The general rule is: if a smaller numeral precedes a larger one, subtract it.
What is the largest number in Roman numerals? ▾
The traditional maximum is 3,999 (MMMCMXCIX) using standard notation. With the vinculum (bar over a letter multiplying by 1,000), numbers up to 3,999,999 are possible. Our converter supports both modes up to 3,999,999.
Why do movies use Roman numerals in copyright dates? ▾
Hollywood studios historically used Roman numerals in film credits to obscure the production year, making reruns easier to sell. The practice became a prestige tradition. For example, a film made in 1999 shows MCMXCIX in end credits. Today it adds a sense of timelessness. Use our converter to quickly decode any movie copyright date.
