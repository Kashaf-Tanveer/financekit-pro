What Is a Percentage Calculator — and How Does It Work?

A percentage calculator is an online tool that helps you compute any kind of percentage-based problem in seconds — whether you need to find what 20% of $500 is, calculate a salary increase of 8%, reverse-engineer the original price before VAT, or figure out the percentage difference between two stock prices.

FinanceKit Pro's free percentage calculator covers seven distinct calculation modes: percentage of a number, X is what % of Y, percentage increase, percentage decrease, reverse percentage, percentage difference, and a combined tip/discount tool. All results update instantly and respect your chosen display currency.

Core Percentage Formulas Explained

% of a Number

Formula: Result = (P ÷ 100) × N
Example: 20% of 500 = (20 ÷ 100) × 500 = 100

X is What % of Y?

Formula: % = (X ÷ Y) × 100
Example: 45 is what % of 180? (45 ÷ 180) × 100 = 25%

Percentage Increase

Formula: % = ((New − Old) ÷ Old) × 100
Example: $80 → $100 = ((100−80) ÷ 80) × 100 = 25%

Percentage Decrease

Formula: % = ((Old − New) ÷ Old) × 100
Example: $5,000 → $4,000 = ((5000−4000) ÷ 5000) × 100 = 20%

Reverse Percentage

Formula (% added): Original = Final ÷ (1 + P/100)
Example: $120 after +20% → 120 ÷ 1.20 = $100

Percentage Difference

Formula: % = |V1−V2| ÷ ((V1+V2)/2) × 100
Example: 90 vs 110 → |90−110| ÷ 100 × 100 = 20%

Real-World Examples — Who Uses a Percentage Calculator?

Investors and traders in the US, UK, Canada, and Australia use percentage calculators daily — to measure portfolio returns, compare stock performance, and size position changes. For example: if your S&P 500 ETF rose from $420 to $487, the gain is ((487−420)÷420)×100 = 15.95%.

Shoppers across the UAE, UK, Germany, and the US rely on the discount calculator: a $250 jacket on 30% sale becomes $175 (saving $75). The tip calculator is especially popular in the US and Canada, where 15–20% gratuities are standard at restaurants.

Business owners and accountants use the reverse percentage tool to strip tax from prices. If a product costs $118 including 18% GST/VAT, the pre-tax price = 118 ÷ 1.18 = $100 exactly.

Students and teachers globally use "X is what % of Y" to convert raw test scores. If a student scores 72 out of 90, that's (72÷90)×100 = 80%.
