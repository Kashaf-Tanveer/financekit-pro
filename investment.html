What Is an Investment Return Calculator?

An investment return calculator shows you how much your money will grow over time based on three key factors: the initial amount invested, the expected annual return rate, and the time period. Advanced features include monthly contributions (SIP mode), compounding frequency, inflation adjustment, and step-up SIP — all of which dramatically affect the final outcome.

How to Use This Investment Calculator

Choose from three modes: Lump Sum (invest once), Monthly SIP (invest a fixed amount every month), or ROI/CAGR (calculate returns on a completed investment). Enter your figures, adjust the sliders, and click Calculate. Results show total value, total profit, ROI percentage, CAGR, and a year-by-year growth table and chart.

Investment Return Formula (CAGR & Compound Interest)

Compound Growth (Lump Sum):
A = P × (1 + r/n)^(n×t)

CAGR:
CAGR = ((Final Value / Initial Value) ^ (1/Years)) − 1

ROI:
ROI = ((Final Value − Initial Investment) / Initial Investment) × 100

Monthly SIP Future Value:
FV = M × [((1 + r/12)^(12t) − 1) / (r/12)] × (1 + r/12)

Where: P = principal, r = annual rate (decimal), n = compounding frequency per year, t = time in years, M = monthly investment, A = final amount.

Frequently Asked Questions — Investment Return Calculator

How do I calculate investment return on stocks in 2026? +
Use the ROI/CAGR tab above. Enter your buy price, current or sell price, holding period in years, and any costs or dividends. The calculator instantly shows your total ROI percentage, annualized CAGR, net profit, and whether your investment beat inflation. Works for stocks, ETFs, mutual funds, real estate, and any other asset class.
What is a good annual return on investment in 2026? +
For context: the S&P 500 has returned approximately 10% annually before inflation over the past 100 years (about 7% after inflation). The FTSE 100 (UK) averages 7-8%, while the Nifty 50 (India) has averaged 12-14%. Savings accounts and government bonds typically yield 3-6% in 2026. Any return consistently above 10-12% after fees is considered strong for long-term investing. Crypto and growth stocks can return far more — but with significantly higher risk.
What is the difference between ROI and CAGR? +
ROI (Return on Investment) is the total percentage gain from start to finish, regardless of time. CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate) is the smoothed annual rate that would produce the same total return. Example: an investment that doubles in 10 years has an ROI of 100%, but a CAGR of 7.18% per year. CAGR is the more useful metric for comparing investments over different time periods.
How much should I invest monthly to become a millionaire? +
Assuming a 10% average annual return (S&P 500 historical average): In 30 years → invest ~$442/month. In 25 years → ~$754/month. In 20 years → ~$1,316/month. In 15 years → ~$2,633/month. Starting earlier is vastly more efficient due to compounding. Use our Monthly SIP calculator above to model your exact scenario in any currency.
Does inflation affect my real investment returns? +
Yes, significantly. If your investment earns 8% annually but inflation is 3%, your real return is approximately 4.85% (not simply 5% — use the Fisher equation: real rate = (1 + nominal) / (1 + inflation) − 1). Our calculator shows both nominal and inflation-adjusted (real) values so you can see what your money is truly worth in today's purchasing power.
What is a Step-Up SIP and should I use one? +
A Step-Up SIP automatically increases your monthly investment by a fixed percentage each year — typically matching your salary growth (e.g., 10% annually). This is one of the most powerful wealth-building strategies: if you invest $500/month with a 10% annual step-up for 20 years at 10% return, you accumulate ~$640,000 vs ~$380,000 without step-up. Use our Monthly SIP tab and enter the annual step-up percentage.
How do taxes affect investment returns in the USA, UK, Canada, and Australia? +
USA: Long-term capital gains tax 0-20% depending on income; use 401(k)/IRA to invest tax-free. UK: 10-20% CGT after £3,000 annual exempt amount (2026); ISA allowance of £20,000/year is tax-free. Canada: 50% of capital gains are taxable as income; TFSA and RRSP are tax-advantaged. Australia: 50% CGT discount if held over 12 months; Superannuation is taxed at 15%. Tax-advantaged accounts dramatically improve long-term returns.
