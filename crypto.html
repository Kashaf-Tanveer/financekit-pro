Understanding Cryptocurrency Market Cap

Market cap is the single most useful number for comparing the relative size of cryptocurrencies. The formula is simple:

Market Cap = Current Price × Circulating Supply

For example, if Bitcoin is trading at $65,000 and there are 19.7 million BTC in circulation, its market cap is approximately $1.28 trillion. A coin priced at $0.001 with a trillion tokens in circulation would still carry a $1 billion market cap — which is why price alone is a misleading metric. Always check market cap when evaluating the size and risk profile of any crypto investment.

How to Use the Fear & Greed Index for Crypto Trading

The Crypto Fear & Greed Index (provided by Alternative.me) is a daily sentiment indicator that measures the emotional state of the crypto market on a scale from 0 to 100:

  • 0–24 — Extreme Fear: Investors are panicking. Historically these periods have preceded strong recoveries.
  • 25–49 — Fear: Market sentiment is cautious. Some investors treat this as a buying opportunity.
  • 50–74 — Greed: Optimism is running high. Watch for overvaluation signs.
  • 75–100 — Extreme Greed: Market is exuberant. Many experienced traders use this as a sell signal.

Warren Buffett's famous maxim — "Be fearful when others are greedy, and greedy when others are fearful" — resonates strongly with crypto traders who use this index as a contrarian tool. It should never be used in isolation but works well alongside price action and on-chain data.

Why Bitcoin Dominance Matters

Bitcoin dominance is the percentage of the total crypto market cap that Bitcoin alone represents. When BTC dominance rises, it typically signals that investors are moving capital from altcoins into Bitcoin — a flight-to-quality move often seen during uncertain periods. When dominance falls, altcoins are often outperforming BTC, which traders call "altcoin season." Monitoring this figure alongside the Fear & Greed Index gives a richer picture of where the market cycle currently stands.
