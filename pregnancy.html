📖 Week-by-Week Pregnancy Guide

What Is a Pregnancy Calculator and How Does It Work?

A pregnancy calculator is a free digital tool that estimates your due date (EDD — Estimated Date of Delivery), how far along you are in your pregnancy, which trimester you're in, and upcoming milestones for both mother and baby. Our free online pregnancy calculator 2026 is used by expecting parents in the USA, UK, Canada, Australia, Germany, UAE, and across 150+ countries.

The Formula: Naegele's Rule (Standard Medical Method)

The globally accepted standard for due date calculation is Naegele's Rule, formulated by German obstetrician Franz Karl Naegele in the 19th century and still used in every hospital worldwide:

Estimated Due Date (EDD) = LMP + 280 days (40 weeks)

Equivalently: EDD = First day of LMP + 9 months + 7 days

Example: If your LMP was January 1, 2026:
EDD = January 1 + 280 days = October 8, 2026

For cycles longer or shorter than 28 days, the ovulation date shifts and so does the due date. Our calculator adjusts for custom cycle lengths (21–45 days) automatically, improving accuracy over the standard formula.

Understanding Trimesters: A Global Guide

Pregnancy is divided into three trimesters, each with distinct developmental milestones:

  • First Trimester (Weeks 1–13): The embryo forms all major organs. Morning sickness, fatigue, and breast tenderness are common. A dating ultrasound is typically done at 10–14 weeks in the US and UK.
  • Second Trimester (Weeks 14–27): Often called the "golden trimester" — energy returns, baby movements begin (~18–22 weeks), and the gender can often be determined. An anatomy scan (20-week scan) is standard in Australia, Canada, and the UK.
  • Third Trimester (Weeks 28–40+): Rapid weight gain, preparation for birth. In the USA, Group B Strep testing occurs at 35–37 weeks. The baby is considered full-term at 39–40 weeks per WHO guidelines.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions — Pregnancy Calculator 2026

How do I calculate my pregnancy due date from my last period? ▾
Add 280 days (40 weeks) to the first day of your last menstrual period (LMP). This is Naegele's Rule, the standard method used by OBGYNs, midwives, and hospitals worldwide. Our free pregnancy calculator does this automatically — just enter your LMP date and get your due date instantly, along with trimester, weeks pregnant, and baby milestones.
How many weeks pregnant am I right now? ▾
Enter the first day of your last menstrual period (LMP) into our pregnancy calculator above. It instantly shows how many weeks and days pregnant you are today, based on today's actual date. Pregnancy is counted from the first day of your LMP, not from conception — which means you are technically "2 weeks pregnant" on the day of conception in standard medical counting.
What are the 3 trimesters and when do they start and end? ▾
The First Trimester runs from Week 1 to Week 13 (approx. months 1–3). The Second Trimester runs from Week 14 to Week 27 (months 4–6). The Third Trimester runs from Week 28 to Week 40+ (months 7–9). Each trimester brings distinct changes in baby development and maternal symptoms. Our calculator shows which trimester you're currently in and how far through it you are.
What is the difference between gestational age and fetal age? ▾
Gestational age counts from the first day of your LMP, which is what doctors always use — it adds approximately 2 weeks before conception. Fetal age (or embryonic age) counts from actual conception and is about 2 weeks less than gestational age. For example, if you're 10 weeks pregnant (gestational), your baby is actually about 8 weeks old (fetal age). Our calculator uses the standard gestational age method.
Can I calculate my due date from my conception date? ▾
Yes! Select the "Conception Date" tab in our calculator above. Add 266 days (38 weeks) to your conception date to get your estimated due date. Conception typically occurs around 10–16 days after your LMP starts (ovulation day varies by cycle length). If you tracked ovulation with LH strips or a fertility monitor, you may know this date quite accurately.
When is my baby's viability date — when can a baby survive outside the womb? ▾
Medical viability begins at approximately 24 weeks of gestational age. At this point, survival with intensive neonatal care (NICU) becomes possible. Survival rates improve significantly from 24 (39%) to 28 weeks (85%) and beyond, according to leading neonatology data from the US, UK, and Australia. Our calculator shows your specific viability date (LMP + 168 days = Week 24). Always consult your OB/GYN for clinical guidance.
How accurate is this pregnancy due date calculator? ▾
Our calculator applies Naegele's Rule — the same standard used by every OB/GYN, midwife, and NHS/AHA guideline globally — and is accurate to the day given a reliable LMP. However, only about 5% of babies are born exactly on their EDD. Most arrive within 2 weeks before or after. A first-trimester ultrasound (7–12 weeks) provides the most accurate dating. Our tool adjusts for custom cycle lengths (21–45 days) beyond the default 28 days for greater accuracy.
What prenatal appointments should I have and when? ▾
Standard prenatal care (US/UK/Canada/Australia): First visit at 8–10 weeks (confirm pregnancy, blood tests, genetic screening discussion). Dating ultrasound at 10–14 weeks. Anatomy scan (20-week scan) at 18–22 weeks. Glucose tolerance test at 24–28 weeks. Group B Strep test at 35–37 weeks (US). Weekly/biweekly visits from 36 weeks onward.
