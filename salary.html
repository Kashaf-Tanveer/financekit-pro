What is a Salary Calculator? How to Calculate Take-Home Pay in 2026

A salary calculator — also known as a paycheck calculator, net pay calculator, or take-home pay calculator — is a free online tool that computes your net salary after all deductions including income tax, Social Security or National Insurance, Medicare, pension contributions, and any other withholdings. FinanceKit Pro's salary calculator supports USA, UK, Canada, Australia, UAE, Germany, Singapore and 170+ other currencies.

Income Tax Brackets by Country (2026)

USA Federal Tax (2026): Income tax is progressive — 10% (up to $11,925), 12% ($11,926–$48,475), 22% ($48,476–$103,350), 24% ($103,351–$197,300), 32% ($197,301–$250,525), 35% ($250,526–$626,350), and 37% above $626,350. Plus 6.2% Social Security (up to $176,100 wage base) and 1.45% Medicare.

UK (2026/27 Tax Year): Personal allowance of £12,570 is tax-free. Basic rate 20% (£12,571–£50,270), higher rate 40% (£50,271–£125,140), additional rate 45% above £125,140. National Insurance: 8% on earnings £12,570–£50,270, 2% above.

Canada Federal (2026): 15% (up to $57,375), 20.5% ($57,376–$114,750), 26% ($114,751–$158,519), 29% ($158,520–$220,000), 33% above $220,000. Plus CPP (5.95%) and EI (1.66%) contributions.

Australia (2026-27): Tax-free threshold $18,200, then 16% to $45,000, 30% to $135,000, 37% to $190,000, and 45% above. Plus 2% Medicare levy and 11.5% mandatory superannuation.

UAE: Zero personal income tax — one of the world's most expat-friendly destinations. All salary is take-home pay (excluding any social contributions for UAE nationals).

Germany (2026): Progressive rates from 14% to 45%, plus solidarity surcharge and church tax where applicable. Contributions to health insurance (~7.3%), pension (9.3%), unemployment (1.3%), and long-term care (1.7%) insurance.

Gross vs Net Salary — What's the Difference?

Gross salary is the total compensation your employer pays before any deductions. Net salary (take-home pay) is what actually lands in your bank account after all taxes and deductions are subtracted. For most employees in high-income countries, net pay is typically 65–80% of gross salary, depending on tax bracket and country.
