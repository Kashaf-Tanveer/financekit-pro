Daily Water Intake Calculator — How Much Water Should You Drink Per Day in 2026?

Knowing exactly how much water you should drink per day is one of the most impactful things you can do for your health. Proper hydration supports nearly every function in the human body — from regulating temperature and lubricating joints, to delivering nutrients to cells and flushing out toxins through urine and sweat.

Our free daily water intake calculator gives you a precise, personalized hydration target based on your body weight, age, biological sex, activity level, and the climate you live in. It is used by health-conscious individuals in the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, Germany, UAE, and 150+ other countries.

The Formula Used in This Calculator

This tool uses a multi-factor formula endorsed by the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) and aligned with WHO hydration guidelines:

Step 1 — Base Intake: Body Weight (kg) × 35 ml
Step 2 — Activity Multiplier: Sedentary ×1.00 | Light ×1.15 | Moderate ×1.30 | Very Active ×1.50 | Extreme ×1.70
Step 3 — Climate Adjustment: Cold −200 ml | Moderate ±0 | Hot +350 ml | Very Hot/Humid +600 ml
Step 4 — Exercise Extra: (Exercise Minutes / 30) × 250 ml
Step 5 — Special Conditions: Pregnant +300 ml | Breastfeeding +700 ml | Sick/Fever +500 ml | Weight-Loss Diet +250 ml
Total Daily Target = Step 1 × Step 2 + Step 3 + Step 4 + Step 5

Example Calculation — 70 kg Moderately Active Adult in a Hot Climate

Let's say you weigh 70 kg, are moderately active (exercise 4 days/week), and live in a hot climate with 45 minutes of daily exercise:

  • Base: 70 × 35 = 2,450 ml
  • Activity (×1.30): 2,450 × 1.30 = 3,185 ml
  • Hot climate: +350 ml → 3,535 ml
  • Exercise (45 min): +375 ml → 3,910 ml
  • Final Target: ~3.9 liters per day (about 15–16 glasses)

How Much Water Should I Drink Per Day for Weight Loss?

Studies published in the Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism show that drinking 500 ml of water increases metabolic rate by 30% for 30–40 minutes. For weight loss, experts recommend drinking 500 ml before each meal to reduce calorie intake, and targeting the upper end of your daily water intake range. Selecting the "Active Weight-Loss Diet" option in our calculator adds the recommended extra intake.

Water Intake by Age Group — General Guidelines

  • Children (4–8 years): 1.2–1.6 liters/day (5–7 glasses)
  • Teens (9–18 years): 1.8–2.6 liters/day (7–10 glasses)
  • Adults (19–50 years): 2.0–3.5 liters/day (8–14 glasses) — varies by activity
  • Adults (51+ years): 1.5–2.5 liters/day — thirst sensation decreases with age
  • Pregnant women: 2.3–3.1 liters/day (10–13 glasses)
  • Breastfeeding mothers: 3.1–3.5 liters/day (13–14 glasses)
💡 Pro Tip: Your urine color is the simplest hydration test. Pale yellow = well hydrated. Dark yellow or amber = drink immediately. Clear urine may mean over-hydration (rare but possible). Aim for pale straw color throughout the day.

Who Uses a Water Intake Calculator?

This calculator is widely used by fitness enthusiasts and athletes optimizing performance; dieters and weight-loss coaches helping clients reduce hunger; office workers in sedentary jobs who forget to hydrate; pregnant and breastfeeding mothers following ACOG guidelines; elderly individuals whose thirst mechanism is reduced; and people living in hot climates such as the Gulf region, South Asia, and Australia who have higher baseline needs due to heat and humidity.

Tips to Reach Your Daily Water Target

  • Start every morning with a full 500 ml glass of water before coffee
  • Carry a 1-liter reusable bottle and aim to refill it 2–3 times
  • Set hourly reminders on your phone using apps like WaterMinder or Hydro Coach
  • Eat water-rich foods: cucumbers (96% water), watermelon (91%), strawberries (91%), lettuce (96%)
  • Drink one glass before every meal — this also reduces calorie intake
  • After any alcoholic drink, follow with one glass of water (1:1 rule)

Frequently Asked Questions — Water Intake Calculator 2026

How much water should I drink per day based on my weight? +
The standard formula is to drink 35 ml of water per kilogram of body weight per day. A 70 kg person needs approximately 2,450 ml (2.45 liters or about 8–10 glasses) daily as a baseline. This increases with exercise, hot weather, pregnancy, or illness. Use our calculator above for your exact personalized target — it factors in all these variables automatically.
Is the "8 glasses per day" rule accurate? +
The "8×8" rule (eight 8-ounce glasses) is a helpful general guideline, but not scientifically precise. The actual amount varies significantly by body weight, physical activity, climate, and health status. A 50 kg sedentary woman in a cold climate may need only 1.5 liters, while a 100 kg male athlete in a hot climate may need 4–5 liters. Our calculator uses a clinically validated weight-based formula for accuracy.
Does drinking more water help with weight loss? +
Yes, research strongly supports water's role in weight management. Drinking water before meals reduces hunger and calorie intake. Studies in the Journal of Clinical Endocrinology show a 30% temporary boost in metabolic rate after drinking 500 ml of cold water. Additionally, staying well-hydrated helps the kidneys flush metabolic waste more efficiently, supports fat metabolism, and reduces water retention (which contributes to bloating and perceived weight gain).
How much extra water should I drink when exercising? +
For moderate exercise lasting 30–60 minutes: drink an additional 250–500 ml during and after. For intense exercise or sessions over an hour: add 500–1,000 ml, and consider an electrolyte drink if sweating heavily. As a general rule, drink 500 ml of water 2 hours before exercise, 150–250 ml every 15–20 minutes during exercise, and 600 ml for every 0.5 kg of body weight lost through sweat post-exercise. Our calculator's exercise slider adds the appropriate extra intake automatically.
How much water should pregnant women drink daily? +
The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) recommends at least 2.3 liters (about 10 cups) per day during pregnancy, rising to 3.1 liters (13 cups) during breastfeeding. Proper hydration during pregnancy reduces the risk of urinary tract infections, prevents constipation, maintains amniotic fluid levels, and supports placental circulation. Morning sickness can also increase fluid loss — so sipping small amounts frequently is recommended in the first trimester.
What are the signs of dehydration I should watch for? +
Early signs of mild dehydration (1–2% body weight): thirst, dark yellow urine, slightly reduced urination, dry mouth. Moderate dehydration (3–5%): headache, fatigue, dizziness, reduced concentration, dry skin, muscle cramps. Severe dehydration (above 5%): rapid heartbeat, confusion, sunken eyes, inability to urinate — requires immediate medical attention. If your urine is dark amber or you haven't urinated in over 8 hours, drink water immediately and consider seeking medical advice.
Does coffee and tea count toward daily water intake? +
Yes — caffeinated beverages contribute to total fluid intake, but their net hydrating effect is lower than plain water. A moderate amount of coffee (1–3 cups/day) provides about 70–80% of its volume as net fluid. Herbal teas count at nearly 100%. Alcohol, however, is a diuretic — it causes you to lose more water than it provides, so always follow alcoholic drinks with water. Fruit-infused water, coconut water, and low-sugar drinks also count toward your daily target.
Can you drink too much water? What is water intoxication? +
Yes, over-hydration (hyponatremia) is possible when drinking extremely large amounts in a short time, which dilutes sodium levels in the blood. This is rare in everyday life but can occur in endurance athletes who drink excessively without replacing electrolytes. Symptoms include nausea, headache, confusion, and in severe cases, seizures. For most healthy adults, the kidneys can process about 800–1,000 ml per hour — drinking beyond that rate over extended periods is the primary risk. Normal daily water targets from our calculator are entirely safe.
