What Is Compound Interest and Why Does It Matter?

Compound interest is the process of earning interest not just on your initial investment (the principal) but also on all previously accumulated interest. Often described as "interest on interest," it is the most powerful force in personal finance. Albert Einstein allegedly called it the "eighth wonder of the world" — and with good reason. Over long time horizons, compound interest transforms modest savings into extraordinary wealth.

Unlike simple interest — which only grows your original deposit — compound interest grows exponentially. The longer your money compounds, the faster it accelerates. This is why starting to invest early, even with small amounts, produces dramatically better outcomes than starting with more money later.

The Compound Interest Formula Explained

Our free compound interest calculator uses the standard financial formula endorsed by the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and used by all major financial institutions worldwide:

A = P × (1 + r/n)^(n×t)

A = Final amount (total value)
P = Principal (initial investment)
r = Annual interest rate (as a decimal — e.g. 8% = 0.08)
n = Compounding frequency per year (12 = monthly, 365 = daily)
t = Time in years

Example: $10,000 at 8% per year, monthly compounding, 10 years:
A = $10,000 × (1 + 0.08/12)^(12×10) = $10,000 × (1.006667)^120 = $22,196
Interest earned = $22,196 − $10,000 = $12,196 (122% gain)

Simple Interest vs Compound Interest — Real Numbers

To understand why compound interest is so powerful, compare $10,000 invested for 30 years at 8% per year. Simple interest: the interest is always calculated on the original $10,000 only, so you earn $800/year × 30 = $24,000 interest, for a final value of $34,000. Compound interest (monthly): the interest is calculated on a growing balance every month. Final value = $109,357 — that's over 3× more than simple interest. The difference, $75,357, is the pure power of compounding — money your money made for you, not from your pocket.
