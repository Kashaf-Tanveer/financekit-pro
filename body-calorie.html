What Is a Calorie Calculator and How Does It Work?

A calorie calculator is a tool that estimates the number of calories your body needs each day based on personal factors like age, gender, height, weight, and physical activity level. Unlike generic "eat 2,000 calories" advice, our free calorie calculator uses scientifically validated equations to give you a personalized daily calorie target — whether your goal is weight loss, muscle gain, or weight maintenance.

Our calculator uses two key metrics: BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) — the calories your body burns at complete rest — and TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) — your total calorie burn including all physical activity. TDEE is the number you should base your diet on.

How to Use This Calorie Calculator

BMR Formula: Mifflin-St Jeor Equation (Most Accurate)

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation (1990) is widely considered the most accurate BMR formula for modern adults and is recommended by the American Dietetic Association. The formula is:

For Men: BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) + 5
For Women: BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) − 161

Example — 30-year-old man, 70 kg, 175 cm:
BMR = (10 × 70) + (6.25 × 175) − (5 × 30) + 5 = 700 + 1093.75 − 150 + 5 = 1,648.75 kcal/day
If moderately active (×1.55): TDEE = 1,648 × 1.55 = 2,555 kcal/day

Harris-Benedict Equation (Revised)

The Harris-Benedict equation, originally developed in 1919 and revised in 1984, is an older but still widely used BMR formula. It tends to be slightly higher than Mifflin-St Jeor for most people:

For Men: BMR = 88.362 + (13.397 × weight kg) + (4.799 × height cm) − (5.677 × age)
For Women: BMR = 447.593 + (9.247 × weight kg) + (3.098 × height cm) − (4.330 × age)

How Many Calories Do You Need to Lose Weight?

To lose body fat, you must consume fewer calories than your TDEE — creating a "calorie deficit." Research shows that approximately 3,500 calories equals 0.45 kg (1 lb) of fat. Therefore:

• A 500 kcal/day deficit leads to approximately 0.5 kg (1 lb) of fat loss per week — safe and sustainable.
• A 250 kcal/day deficit leads to ~0.25 kg/week — very gradual, easy to maintain.
• A 1,000 kcal/day deficit can lose ~1 kg/week — aggressive, not recommended for most people without medical supervision.

The safest approach for most adults is a moderate deficit of 300–500 kcal/day, achieved through a combination of reduced calorie intake and increased physical activity.
