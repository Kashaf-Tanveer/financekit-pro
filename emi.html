Complete Guide: How to Use the Free EMI Calculator 2026

An EMI (Equated Monthly Installment) is a fixed monthly payment made by a borrower to a lender. Our free EMI calculator uses the industry-standard reducing balance formula: EMI = P × r × (1+r)^N ÷ ((1+r)^N − 1), where P is the principal, r is the monthly interest rate, and N is the number of months.

Frequently Asked Questions — EMI Calculator

What is EMI and how is it calculated? +
EMI stands for Equated Monthly Installment — a fixed monthly payment made by a borrower to a lender. It is calculated using: EMI = P × r × (1+r)^N ÷ ((1+r)^N − 1), where P = principal loan amount, r = monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and N = number of months. Each EMI covers both interest and a portion of the principal, with the interest portion decreasing over time.
What happens if I miss a loan EMI payment? +
Missing an EMI has serious consequences: late payment charges (typically 1-3% per month on overdue amounts), credit score damage affecting future loan eligibility, and after 3+ consecutive missed payments, the loan may be classified as non-performing with recovery proceedings. Always contact your lender proactively if you anticipate payment difficulty — most banks offer restructuring options.
Can I change my EMI amount during the loan tenure? +
Yes — through balance transfer (moving to another lender with lower rates), loan restructuring (negotiating new tenure or rate with your current bank), or making prepayments to reduce principal and requesting a revised lower EMI. For floating rate loans, the EMI automatically adjusts when benchmark interest rates change. Check your loan agreement for specific prepayment terms.
Which is better — shorter loan tenure or lower EMI? +
Shorter tenure = higher EMI but significantly lower total interest (saving you thousands). Longer tenure = lower EMI but higher total cost over the life of the loan. General rule: keep total EMI commitments below 40-50% of monthly income. If you can comfortably afford a higher EMI, shorter tenure is almost always the financially smarter choice. Use our Extra Payment calculator to see the impact.
How does home loan EMI differ from personal loan EMI? +
The EMI formula is identical, but the loans differ in: interest rates (home loans typically 4-9% vs personal loans 8-25%), tenure (home loans 15-30 years vs personal loans 1-7 years), collateral (home loans are secured against the property; personal loans are usually unsecured), and loan amounts. Home loans often qualify for tax deductions in many countries.
How to calculate EMI for a loan with processing fee included? +
Processing fees (typically 0.5-2% of loan amount) are usually deducted upfront by the lender. To calculate the effective EMI including the processing fee: add the processing fee to your effective principal (you receive less than the sanctioned amount but pay EMI on the full amount). For example, on a $100,000 loan with 1% processing fee ($1,000), you receive $99,000 but pay EMI on $100,000 — making the effective interest rate higher than stated.
