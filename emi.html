What is an EMI Calculator and How Does It Work?

An EMI (Equated Monthly Installment) calculator is an online financial tool that helps borrowers calculate the fixed monthly payment required to repay a loan over a specified tenure. Whether you are planning a home loan in the USA, UK, Canada or Australia, a car loan, or a personal loan, this free EMI calculator gives you an instant, accurate result — no sign-up required.

EMI Formula Explained

The EMI is calculated using the reducing-balance (diminishing balance) method, which is the global banking standard:

EMI = [P × r × (1+r)ⁿ] ÷ [(1+r)ⁿ − 1]

Where:
P = Principal loan amount
r = Monthly interest rate = Annual Rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100
n = Loan tenure in months (years × 12)

For example, on a $300,000 home loan at 7% for 30 years: r = 7/12/100 = 0.00583, n = 360 months. EMI = [$300,000 × 0.00583 × (1.00583)³⁶⁰] ÷ [(1.00583)³⁶⁰ − 1] = $1,995.91 per month. Total interest paid over 30 years = $418,527.

Home Loan EMI Calculator — USA, UK, Canada, Australia

In the United States, the average 30-year fixed mortgage rate typically ranges from 6.5% to 7.5% (2026). A $400,000 home loan at 7% for 30 years results in a monthly EMI of approximately $2,661 and total interest of $557,960. In the United Kingdom, with a £250,000 mortgage at 5.5% for 25 years, the monthly repayment is approximately £1,532. In Canada, a CAD 500,000 mortgage at 5.75% for 25 years costs roughly CAD 3,136/month.

Car Loan EMI — USA, UK, Germany, UAE

Auto loan interest rates are generally higher than mortgage rates. In the USA, typical car loan rates range from 7% to 12% for 36–72 months. A $35,000 car loan at 9% for 5 years (60 months) results in a monthly EMI of $726. In the UAE, car loans are commonly available at 2.99%–5.5% flat rate (equivalent to approximately 5.5%–10% reducing balance). This EMI calculator uses the internationally standard reducing balance method for accurate comparisons.

📖 Loan Glossary

EMI — Fixed monthly payment covering principal + interest.
Principal — The original loan amount borrowed.
Tenure — Duration of the loan in months or years.
Amortization — Schedule showing how each EMI splits between principal and interest.
Prepayment — Extra payment made to reduce outstanding principal.
Processing Fee — One-time charge by lender, typically 0.5%–2% of loan.
LTV — Loan-to-Value ratio. Most lenders cap at 80%–90% for mortgages.
