Frequently Asked Questions

What is EMI and how is it calculated? +
EMI stands for Equated Monthly Installment — a fixed payment made by a borrower to a lender each month. It is calculated using: EMI = P × r × (1+r)^N ÷ ((1+r)^N − 1), where P = principal, r = monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and N = number of months. Each EMI covers both interest and a portion of the principal.
What happens if I miss an EMI payment? +
Missing an EMI has serious consequences: late payment charges (1-2% per month on overdue amount), credit score damage reported to SECP and CIBs, and after 3 consecutive missed payments, the loan may be classified as Non-Performing. Always contact your bank proactively if you anticipate difficulty.
Can I change my EMI amount during the loan tenure? +
Yes — through balance transfer (moving to another bank with lower rates), loan restructuring (negotiating new tenure/rate), making prepayments to reduce principal, or for floating rate loans, EMI automatically adjusts when KIBOR rates change. Check your loan agreement for specific prepayment terms.
Which is better — shorter tenure or lower EMI? +
Shorter tenure = higher EMI but significantly lower total interest (saving lakhs). Longer tenure = lower EMI but higher total cost. Rule of thumb: keep total EMI below 40-50% of monthly income. If you can afford a higher EMI, shorter tenure is almost always better. Use the "Extra Payment" calculator above to see how even small additional payments dramatically cut your interest.
How does home loan EMI differ from personal loan EMI? +
The formula is the same, but they differ in: interest rate (home loans 8-13% vs personal loans 14-25%), tenure (home loans up to 20-30 years vs personal loans 1-5 years), collateral (home loans secured against property; personal loans usually unsecured), and loan amounts. Home loans may also qualify for tax deductions in Pakistan under certain conditions.
How to calculate EMI for a loan with processing fee? +
Processing fees (0.5-2% of loan amount) are usually deducted upfront. To calculate the effective EMI: add the processing fee to your effective principal (since you receive less than sanctioned). For example, on a ₨10 lakh loan with 1% processing fee (₨10,000), you receive ₨9,90,000 but pay EMI on ₨10,00,000 — making the effective rate higher than stated.
