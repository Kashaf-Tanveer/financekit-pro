What Is an Inflation Calculator? How to Calculate the Real Value of Money

An inflation calculator measures the change in purchasing power of money over time using Consumer Price Index (CPI) data. Inflation refers to the gradual rise in the price of goods and services — meaning a dollar, pound, or euro today buys less than it did 10 or 20 years ago. This free tool helps you see exactly how much purchasing power has been lost or gained between any two years from 1960 to 2026.

The Inflation Formula (CPI Method)

The standard method to adjust for inflation uses the Consumer Price Index:

Formula

Adjusted Value = Original Amount × (CPI in End Year ÷ CPI in Start Year)

For example: If you had $10,000 in the year 2000 and the US CPI was 172.2 then and is roughly 315 in 2026, the inflation-adjusted value would be: $10,000 × (315 / 172.2) = $18,292. This means you would need $18,292 today to have the same purchasing power as $10,000 in 2000.

Real-World Examples

Example 1 — USA: $1,000 in 2000 vs 2026

Due to US inflation averaging around 2.5–3% per year, $1,000 in 2000 is equivalent to approximately $1,750–$1,850 in 2026. If you kept that $1,000 in cash under a mattress, you effectively lost ~45% of its real purchasing power.

Example 2 — UK: £50,000 Salary in 2010 vs 2026

A £50,000 salary in 2010 — after accounting for UK CPI inflation — required approximately £72,000–£75,000 in 2026 to maintain the same standard of living. This illustrates the importance of salary reviews that keep pace with inflation.

Example 3 — Canada: $500,000 Home in 2015 vs 2026

A $500,000 home purchase in 2015 Canada would need to be worth around $650,000–$680,000 in 2026 just to break even against inflation — before factoring in real appreciation.

HR professionals use it to set cost-of-living salary adjustments. Retirees and savers use it to understand if their savings are growing faster than inflation. What is the difference between nominal and real value of money? ▾
Nominal value is the face value of money (e.g. $1,000 today). Real value is the purchasing-power-adjusted worth — what that $1,000 could actually buy compared to another time period. The inflation calculator converts nominal to real using the CPI formula. This distinction matters greatly for investors: a 6% nominal return in a 4% inflation environment only yields a 2% real return.
How does the CPI inflation calculator work? ▾
The CPI (Consumer Price Index) measures the average price level of a representative basket of goods and services — food, housing, transport, healthcare, education and more. Our calculator compares the CPI in your chosen start year versus end year: Adjusted Amount = Original Amount × (CPI End Year ÷ CPI Start Year). If CPI rose from 100 to 180 over that period, $500 becomes $900 in equivalent purchasing power.

What is inflation and why does it affect personal finance? ▾
Inflation is the sustained increase in the general price level of an economy. At 3% annual inflation, something that costs $100 today will cost $103 next year and $134 in 10 years. For personal finance, this means: savings in accounts earning below the inflation rate lose real value, fixed-rate bonds underperform, and salaries that don't keep up with CPI result in falling living standards. Smart investors seek inflation-beating returns via equities, real estate, TIPS, and other hedges.
